  <!-- ================= TITLE ================= -->
  <h1 class="project__title">Promoting Efficient Exploration in Predictive Musculoskeletal Simulation by Adopting a Stretch Reflex System</h1>
  <p class="project__authors">Jun Hyuk Lee, Jooeun Ahn</p>
  <p class="project__affil">Sports Engineering Lab, Seoul National University &middot; WCB 2026 (poster)</p>

  <!-- ================= PROBLEM ================= -->
  <section class="project__section">
    <h2 class="project__heading">The Inefficient Exploration Problem</h2>

    <p>Predictive musculoskeletal simulation tries to generate human movement from first principles, without copying recorded motion. In practice, two difficulties show up again and again: it is hard to make the model move the way you intend, and it is hard to make the optimization or training converge within a realistic time budget. Both stem from the same root cause, which I call the <strong>inefficient exploration problem</strong>.</p>

    <p>A simple, single-joint example makes the issue concrete. The joint is driven by two flexors (<span class="project__mono">f1</span>, <span class="project__mono">f2</span>) and two extensors (<span class="project__mono">e1</span>, <span class="project__mono">e2</span>).</p>

    <figure class="project__figure">
      <img src="{{ base_path }}/images/wcb2026/fig-a-system.png" alt="Single-joint system with two flexors and two extensors" />
      <figcaption>(a) A single-joint system actuated by two flexors (f1, f2) and two extensors (e1, e2).</figcaption>
    </figure>

    <p>The core problem is this: <strong>even when the policy actively explores in the muscle-activation space, that exploration does not translate into meaningful exploration in joint space.</strong> There are two reasons.</p>

    <p>First, muscle-activation profiles are <strong>independent across time</strong>. Without any temporal structure, the muscles simply flicker on and off, and the limb barely moves.</p>

    <figure class="project__figure">
      <img src="{{ base_path }}/images/wcb2026/fig-b-flickering.png" alt="Independent, flickering muscle activations over time" />
      <figcaption>(b) Time-independent activations only flicker, producing no coherent joint motion.</figcaption>
    </figure>

    <p>Second, there is <strong>no structural correlation</strong> between muscles: a muscle cannot coordinate with its own synergists. As a result, active muscle-space exploration never becomes joint-space exploration, the policy keeps repeating the same behavior throughout training, and it often fails to converge in a realistic time.</p>

    <figure class="project__figure">
      <!-- Replace fig-d-repeating.png with the "repeating behavior" GIF -->
      <img src="{{ base_path }}/images/wcb2026/fig-d-repeating.png" alt="Policy repeating the same behavior throughout training" />
      <figcaption>(d) Without temporal or structural correlation, the policy keeps repeating the same behavior. <em>[GIF]</em></figcaption>
    </figure>
  </section>

  <!-- ================= STRETCH REFLEX SYSTEM ================= -->
  <section class="project__section">
    <h2 class="project__heading">The Stretch Reflex System</h2>

    <p>The fix is to let each muscle's excitation emerge from its own mechanical state through a <strong>stretch reflex</strong>, and to set the control variables to the reflex threshold and gains rather than the raw activations.</p>

    <div class="project__equation">
      $$\text{Excitation} = \Big[\, G_{\text{tonic}}\,(L - \lambda) \;+\; G_{\text{phasic}}\,[v]_+ \,\Big]_0^1$$
    </div>

    <p>where \([x]_0^1 = \mathrm{clip}(x, 0, 1)\), \(G_{\text{tonic}}\) is the tonic gain, \(G_{\text{phasic}}\) is the phasic gain, \(L\) is the muscle-fiber length, \(\lambda\) is the reflex threshold, and \(v\) is the muscle-fiber velocity.</p>

    <ul class="project__points">
      <li><strong>The control variables are only \(\lambda\), \(G_{\text{tonic}}\), and \(G_{\text{phasic}}\).</strong> Excitation emerges from the muscle's own state \((L, v)\), rather than being commanded directly.</li>
      <li><strong>The tonic term \(G_{\text{tonic}}(L - \lambda)\) follows Feldman's \(\lambda\)-model</strong> [1]: the limb converges toward an equilibrium point set by the threshold.</li>
      <li><strong>The phasic term \(G_{\text{phasic}}[v]_+\) acts as a damper</strong>: excitation proportional to lengthening velocity resists rapid stretch.</li>
      <li><strong>It is physiologically grounded.</strong> The stretch reflex is a well-established spinal mechanism [2], not a modeling artifact.</li>
    </ul>

    <p>With this reflex in place, muscle activations become <strong>temporally correlated</strong>, and <strong>structural correlation emerges</strong> as synergistic activation profiles.</p>

    <figure class="project__figure">
      <img src="{{ base_path }}/images/wcb2026/fig-c-correlated.png" alt="Temporally correlated, synergistic muscle activations over time" />
      <figcaption>(c) Under the stretch reflex, activations become temporally correlated and synergistic.</figcaption>
    </figure>

    <p>Because the muscles now move together, diverse movements are explored from the earliest stage of training, and the optimization converges faster than prior approaches.</p>

    <figure class="project__figure">
      <!-- Replace fig-e-diverse.png with the "diverse movements" GIF -->
      <img src="{{ base_path }}/images/wcb2026/fig-e-diverse.png" alt="Diverse movements explored from early training" />
      <figcaption>(e) Diverse movements are explored from the early stage of training. <em>[GIF]</em></figcaption>
    </figure>
  </section>

  <!-- ================= RESULT ================= -->
  <section class="project__section">
    <h2 class="project__heading">Sprinting Emerges from a Simple Reward</h2>

    <p>The model is trained purely with reinforcement learning, using a <strong>single exponential forward-velocity reward and no other reward terms</strong>. From this alone, a human-like sprinting motion emerges within a few hours of training.</p>

    <figure class="project__figure">
      <!-- Replace sprint-sequence.png with the sprinting sequence GIF -->
      <img src="{{ base_path }}/images/wcb2026/sprint-sequence.png" alt="Emergent human-like sprinting motion sequence" />
      <figcaption>Human-like sprinting emerges from a single exponential velocity reward. <em>[GIF]</em></figcaption>
    </figure>

    <ul class="project__points">
      <li>Peak forward speed reaches <strong>6.1 m/s</strong>.</li>
      <li>Without the stretch reflex system, training does not converge in a realistic time.</li>
      <li>No imitation (reference data), no dimension reduction (synergies), no symmetry mirroring, and no external exploration tricks are used.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
